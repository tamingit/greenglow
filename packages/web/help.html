GreenGlow Connect user guide

GreenGlow shows the same IBM i screens you use in IBM i Access Client Solutions (ACS), in your browser. The programs on the IBM i don't change: you sign on, type and press the same keys. GreenGlow adds tabs, copy and paste, sharing and macros, and can give the screens a cleaner look.

Signing in and connecting

  1. Open GreenGlow in Edge or Chrome. The first time, sign in with your work Microsoft account.
  2. The Disconnected card shows the IBM i systems you may use. With several, each is a tile: click one, or move between them with the arrow keys and press Enter. The system you used last has the focus. With one system, press Connect to it.
  3. The IBM i sign-on screen appears. Sign on with your IBM i user and password, as in ACS.

Each system shows a lock that says how the connection to it is protected:

  • Green: TLS, and the IBM i's certificate is checked.
  • Yellow: encrypted, but the certificate isn't checked.
  • Red: plain Telnet, not encrypted. Only test systems are set up this way.

If no system is listed, ask an administrator to give you access. Your IBM i password is typed into the IBM i screen and never kept by GreenGlow; no one you share a session with ever sees it.

The screen and the top bar

From left to right, the top bar holds:

  • Tabs, one for each session, named after the screen's title and coloured for its system. See Several sessions.
  • Who has the keyboard, when someone else is in the session with you. See Sharing a session.
  • The status: what the session is doing, with a coloured dot.
  • Full screen. In full screen, keys such as Ctrl+W and F11 go to the IBM i instead of the browser.
  • The menu (☰).

The menu holds:

  • Look: Classic, GreenGlow or Night Shift. See Looks and colours.
  • Customize colours…
  • Rule line: Off, Crosshair, Row or Column. A red line through the cursor, as in ACS.
  • Screen size: Scale, Stretch or Fixed. Scale makes the screen as big as the window allows while keeping its shape. Stretch fills the window, widening or narrowing the characters to do it. Fixed keeps the Font size you choose under it; if the screen is then bigger than the window, scroll to see the rest. With Scale or Stretch, the screen resizes to fit beside an open side panel.
  • Colour for the system: your own colour for its tab, or Use the system's colour.
  • Share this session… and Disconnect, while connected.
  • Show sharing panel and Show keys panel.
  • Administration, for administrators.
  • Full screen and Show session log.
  • Help: this guide.
  • Sign out.

The line under the screen is the 5250 status line:

  • X SYSTEM: the IBM i is working; keys you type now wait their turn (type-ahead).
  • X II and a number: a keyboard error, with its message on the bottom line. Press Left Ctrl (Reset) to clear it.
  • INS: insert mode. Type-ahead: keys waiting to be sent.
  • The cursor's row and column.

The mouse, copy and paste

  • Click a position to move the cursor there.
  • Drag to select a block of the screen. Shift+click extends it.
  • Turn the mouse wheel to page through a list (Page Down and Page Up). On a touch screen, swipe up or down.
  • Click a function key legend such as F3=Exit to press that key.
  • Right-click for Copy, Paste, Select all and, on a list, the table copies.

Copy

Ctrl+C copies the selected block as it shows, without trailing blanks. Password fields copy as blanks.

Paste

Ctrl+V types the text from the cursor as if you typed it. Each new line goes to the same column one row down, and a tab moves to the next field. Paste never presses Enter, so check the screen and press Enter yourself. If the IBM i refuses a character, pasting stops there and the status says why.

Copy a table

On a screen with a list:

  • Copy table (Alt+Shift+C) copies the page showing.
  • Copy entire table (Alt+C) presses Page Down for you until the last page (up to 200 pages) and copies every row. Esc stops it.

The rows are copied with tabs between columns, so they paste into Excel as columns. The option column is left out.

Several sessions

Each tab is its own IBM i session, up to 10. + or Alt+N opens another; Alt+1 to Alt+0 switch between them. A tab's name is the title of its screen. Close a tab with its ×, a middle-click, or Delete while the tabs have the focus. How many sessions you may have on a system is set by your administrator; the IBM i itself allows three per user, ACS included.

Sessions wait for you

Closing the browser, or losing the network, doesn't end your sessions. GreenGlow keeps them open for a few minutes, so a reload or a new window comes back to the same IBM i jobs, each in its tab. When you open GreenGlow it rejoins your sessions that no other window is showing.

When a tab has no session, the Disconnected card lists your open sessions: when each was opened, the system, the IBM i user, the screen and how many browsers show it.

  • Join shows the session in this tab.
  • Take over brings your session here when it is open in as many browsers as allowed; the other browser lets it go.
  • End ends the session on the server.
  • Tick several and press Join selected to open each in a tab.

Disconnecting and signing off

The cleanest way to finish is to sign off the IBM i (for example SIGNOFF, or the sign-off option on your menu). GreenGlow then closes the tab by itself.

Disconnect in the menu, or closing a tab, while you're still signed on asks what to do:

  • Stay connected (or Esc): changes nothing.
  • End session: drops your IBM i job. The program you're in may end with an error, so sign off first when you can.
  • Leave it running: the session waits on the server for a few minutes for you to join it again.

When a session ends by itself

A message says why. The most common:

Message Meaning
Session ended The IBM i ended the session, usually because you signed off.
Connection to the IBM i lost The IBM i or the network dropped the connection. Connect again.
Connection to GreenGlow lost Your browser lost GreenGlow. If the session is still open, join it from the list.
GreenGlow restarted The GreenGlow server restarted.
Ended elsewhere You ended it from another tab or browser.
Opened elsewhere Another of your browsers took the session over.
Owner left, Sharing stopped In a session shared with you: the owner disconnected or stopped sharing.
Connection too slow Your browser fell behind. The session is still open: join it again.

If a session ended while a program was running, signing on again on the same device offers to reconnect the job. The program may have ended, and may take more than one try to start again.

Side panels

Four panels pull out from tabs on the right edge of the window, one at a time: Keys (Alt+K), Share (Alt+S), Macros (Alt+M) and Printer (Alt+P, see Printer output). The screen makes room for the panel. Press the same keys again, or ×, to close it.

The Keys panel has F1 to F24, Field Exit, Erase Input, Page Up, Page Down and Enter. Other keys adds the rest: Help, Print, Clear, Attention, System Request, Dup, Erase EOF, Reset and more. Each button sends that one key, and the cursor stays on the screen.

Sharing a session

You can let a colleague or the Helpdesk see your session, or work in it, without giving them your password.

  1. Choose Share this session… in the menu, or + on the Share panel.
  2. Under Add a person, type a name and pick them.
  3. Choose their access: View-Only watches; Full Access can take the keyboard and works as you, under your IBM i user. Give Full Access only to someone you would let use your keyboard.
  4. Press Create link, then Copy link and send it to them. Only the people you named can use it.

Someone with GreenGlow open sees a card in the corner when you share with them, and can join with Alt+J. Sessions shared with them are also listed on their Disconnected card and Share panel.

The Share panel shows:

  • Who has the keyboard. Only one person types at a time. Someone with Full Access presses Take control to type, and Return control to give it back.
  • Shared with: each person, with a light (green: watching now; yellow: in GreenGlow but not watching; hollow: not in GreenGlow). Click the keyboard to switch between View-Only and Full Access; × stops sharing with them. Changes take effect at once.
  • Sessions shared with you, to join, leave or take off your list.

People you share with can see the session only while you're in it: when you disconnect, they are sent away until you come back. Passwords never show, for anyone.

Macros

A macro replays keys and field entries you recorded, screen by screen. Macros are kept on the server, so they follow you to any browser.

Record

  1. Go to the screen the macro should start on.
  2. On the Macros panel press ●, name the macro and press Record.
  3. Work as usual. The Macros tab shows a red dot while recording.
  4. Press Stop to save it, or Cancel.

What you type into a password field is never recorded: the macro asks for it when it plays.

Play

Press ▶, or the keys you gave it. It must start on the screen it was recorded on, and stops if a screen isn't what it expects. Esc stops it.

Keys, ACS macros and sharing

  • ⌨ gives a macro a key combination: Alt+Shift and a letter or digit.
  • ⇩ imports a macro saved by ACS (.mac); ⇧ exports one for ACS. A macro that holds a typed password can't be imported: make it a prompt in ACS first.
  • ⇪ shares a macro with people or groups you're in. They can play it; only you can change it.
  • ✎ renames and × deletes your own macros.

Printer output

Your spooled files (what your programs printed) on the system of the tab showing, to read, save or print as PDFs, as Printer Output does in ACS.

  1. Press Alt+P, or the Printer tab on the right edge.
  2. The first time, sign on with your IBM i user and password. The user is filled in from your session on that system. A wrong password counts toward disabling your profile, as at the sign-on screen. GreenGlow doesn't keep the password: after 15 minutes unused, or Sign off, it asks again.
  3. Your spooled files are listed, newest first, with their pages, job and output queue. Move with the arrow keys and press Enter, or click one: it opens as a PDF in a new browser tab. Print it from there (Ctrl+P) or save it.
  4. Refresh lists them again.

The panel shows the system of the tab you're on. Another tab on the same system, signed on as the same IBM i user, shows the same list without signing on again; a tab on another system, or signed on as another user, asks you to sign on there. Only your own spooled files are listed. Reports print as text pages; files made for AFP printers (shown with their type, such as *AFPDS) can't be shown here: open those in ACS.

Looks and colours

Choose a look in the menu. Each browser remembers its own choice.

  • Classic looks like ACS. Nothing is added.
  • GreenGlow keeps every screen as it is but draws it more clearly: input fields as boxes, function keys as buttons you can click, lists as tables with a More… button, windows as dialogs, and messages as banners. The status line says what the field at the cursor takes, for example Numbers only · 10 positions · Required.
  • Night Shift is the GreenGlow look on a dark background.

Customize colours… changes the colours of the look you're using, in this browser. Save to a file keeps them, to load in another browser or give to a colleague with Load from a file…. Use the look's colours goes back.

Install it as an app

In Edge or Chrome, press the install icon at the right of the address bar (or find Install in the browser's menu, under Apps). GreenGlow then opens in its own window from the Start menu or taskbar, like ACS. It still needs the network.

When something goes wrong

  • "Device in use": an earlier session still holds your device name. Try again once the IBM i drops it, or Use a spare name now.
  • Keyboard locked (X II): read the message on the bottom line, then press Left Ctrl (Reset).
  • Session log (in the menu) lists what happened to the connection. Include it when you report a problem.
  • The Classic look shows the screen exactly as ACS does, if another look ever gets in the way.
